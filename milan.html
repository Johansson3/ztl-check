Italy · Milan

A traffic fine from Milan

Milan is the one city where "ZTL fine" is usually the wrong description. It runs two separate schemes with different logic, and which one caught you decides what you can argue.

Area C - the central charge zone

A small zone covering the historic centre, active on weekdays from early morning until the evening, free at weekends and on public holidays. Entry is not forbidden; it is paid for, with a daily ticket of a few euros that covers as many entries as you like until the zone closes that evening.

Because it is a payment scheme, the failure is usually administrative rather than deliberate. The driver entered and never paid, paid after the deadline, or paid and attached the payment to the wrong plate. That last one is common with a hire car, where the plate on the rental agreement is the only record the driver has.

Area B - the emission zone

A far larger area covering most of the city. The restriction here is about what the vehicle emits, not whether you bought access, and it runs on weekdays. There is no ticket. Either the vehicle was allowed in or it was not, and no payment after the fact changes that.

Area C sits inside Area B, which produces a trap worth understanding: a vehicle that fails the Area B rules is excluded from Area C too, even with a valid Area C ticket. Paying does not buy your way past an emissions rule.

Read the letter for which scheme it names The grounds that work in one do not work in the other. If it names Area C, a payment receipt with the right plate on it matters, and so does the timestamp. If it names Area B, a receipt is irrelevant; the question is what the vehicle was.

If it was a rental car

With Area B, the vehicle's emission class was the rental company's choice, not yours. That is not a ground against the city, which fines the vehicle regardless of who chose it. It is, though, worth raising with the rental company: you were handed a car that could not lawfully be driven in the city you hired it to visit, and that is a conversation about their service, not about Italian traffic law. Keep it separate from the appeal.

What stays the same as everywhere else

The timing. Five days for the 30% discount, 60 days to appeal to the Prefect, and the same factual grounds - wrong plate, wrong date or time, wrong vehicle - carry the same weight here as anywhere in Italy. So does the risk: a failed appeal to the Prefect costs more than paying.

Where a Milan appeal goes

To the Prefecture of Milan, in Corso Monforte, within 60 days of the date you were notified. The generator fills in that address for you.
