Italy · Milan

A traffic fine from Milan

Milan is the one city where “ZTL fine” is usually the wrong description. It runs two separate schemes, and which one caught you changes what you can argue.

Area C — the central charge zone

A small central zone where entry has to be paid for. The failure here is usually administrative rather than deliberate: the driver entered and never paid, or paid late. A rental car makes this worse, because the payment has to be attached to the right plate.

Area B — the emission zone

A much larger area covering most of the city, where the restriction is about what the vehicle emits rather than whether you bought access. You cannot buy your way in after the fact: either the vehicle was allowed or it was not. If you were driving a rental, the vehicle's class was the rental company's choice, not yours — which is worth raising with them, though it is not a ground against the city.

Read the letter for which scheme it names The grounds that work in one are not the grounds that work in the other. If the letter names Area C, a payment receipt matters. If it names Area B, it does not.

What stays the same

The timing does. Five days for the discount, 60 days to appeal, and the same factual grounds — wrong plate, wrong date or time, wrong vehicle — carry the same weight here as anywhere else in Italy. And as everywhere, a failed appeal to the Prefect leaves you paying double.
