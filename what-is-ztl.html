The basics

What a ZTL is, and why the fine found you

ZTL stands for Zona a Traffico Limitato - a restricted traffic zone. Italian cities use them to keep cars out of historic centres, and cameras at every entrance read the plate of everything that drives in.

Where they existMore than 200 Italian cities, including Rome, Florence, Bologna and Milan
Typical fine€80 to €300 per entry
How it's enforcedAutomatic plate-reading cameras at each entrance
The signA white circle with a red border, usually with “ZTL” and the active hours underneath

Why tourists get caught

The sign is small, it is often placed where you are already committed to the turn, and satellite navigation routes straight through zones it does not know about. By the time you understand what the sign meant, the camera has already read your plate.

Each entry is a separate fine If you drove in and out three times over a weekend looking for your hotel, that is three fines, not one.

How it reaches you months later

This is the part that surprises people. If you were driving a rental car, the city contacts the rental company, which identifies you as the driver and charges you an administrative fee - usually €40 to €60 - for passing your details on. That charge is not the fine.

The actual fine is then posted to your home address. For a foreign-registered vehicle the notification can legally arrive up to 360 days after the entry; for a rental, the city has to reach the rental company within 90 days. So a letter in autumn about a trip in spring is entirely normal.

What you can do about it

Three things decide the answer: which document you are holding, how many days have passed since it reached you, and whether anything on it is factually wrong. Pay within five days and the amount drops by 30%. You have 60 days from notification to appeal - but appealing without a ground is worse than paying.
