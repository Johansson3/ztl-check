Before you write anything

An appeal is not a free shot

If an appeal to the Prefect fails, the fine is doubled automatically — and you only find out when the refusal arrives. Go to the Giudice di Pace instead and you pay a court fee, plus costs if you lose. So the first question is never “how do I appeal”. It is “do I have a ground”.

Grounds that win

They have one thing in common: they are factual, and you can prove them with a document you already have. No argument about fairness is involved.

  • Wrong number plate. The plate on the letter is not the car you drove. Your rental agreement settles it.
  • Wrong date or time. The entry is logged for a day you were not in the city, or not in that car.
  • Wrong vehicle. The make or model on the letter does not match what you were driving.
  • Notified too late. For a foreign-registered vehicle the notification has to reach you within 360 days of the entry. Past that, the ground stands on its own, regardless of whether you really drove in.

The ground that needs evidence

Your hotel was supposed to register the plate Hotels inside a ZTL can register a guest's plate so the entry is authorised. When they forget, appeals succeed in roughly a third of cases — but only with the email where you sent them the plate and their reply confirming it. Without that paper trail, this is not a ground, it is a story.

Grounds that do not win

  • “I didn't see the sign.”
  • “My sat-nav sent me in.”
  • “I'm a tourist and didn't know the rule.”
  • “I was only in the zone for a minute.”

All of these are true for most people who get fined, and none of them change the outcome. If this is your situation, the cheapest path is to pay — and to pay quickly, because of the discount.
