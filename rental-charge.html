The most expensive misunderstanding

That charge from your rental company is not the fine

It is an administration fee, usually €40 to €60, for identifying you to the Italian authorities and passing on your name and address. The fine itself is still coming.

Why this costs people money They see the charge, assume the matter is closed, and bin the official letter when it arrives two months later. Unpaid fines are increased, and collection follows across borders.

The order things happen in

  1. Camera reads the plate as you enter the zone.
  2. The city asks the rental company who was driving. It must reach them within 90 days.
  3. The rental company gives them your details and charges you its fee. This is what hit your card.
  4. The official fine is posted to your home address — for a foreign-registered vehicle, legally up to 360 days after the entry.

What to do now

Nothing, except watch the post. Do not pay anything twice, and do not assume a quiet few months means it went away. When the envelope with the Italian city's letterhead arrives, that is the document that matters, and your 60-day clock starts then.

Can you dispute the rental company's fee?

Sometimes, and this is a different fight from the fine itself. The fine is a matter between you and the city. The fee is a consumer matter between you and the company: whether it was in the contract you signed, whether the amount is proportionate, and whether they actually forwarded the documentation to you. That one goes through ordinary consumer channels, and it is the easier of the two to win.
