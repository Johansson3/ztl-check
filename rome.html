A ZTL fine from Rome

Rome is the city where a single weekend most often produces more than one letter, and the reason is structural. It does not have a restricted zone. It has six of them, side by side, each with its own boundary, its own hours and its own file number.

The six zones

Centro Storico, Tridente, Trastevere, San Lorenzo, Testaccio and Appia Antica. They are not one perimeter with one clock. Centro Storico and Tridente run through the working day. Trastevere closes in the morning and again at night. San Lorenzo and Testaccio are night zones and nothing else. Several of the night restrictions run only from spring to autumn, and in August the night zones stop altogether.

The practical effect: a street that was open when you drove down it on Tuesday afternoon can be closed at the same hour on Saturday, and a zone you crossed safely in August can catch you in September.

Check each letter separately Different file numbers mean different clocks. If three letters arrived, you have three five-day discount windows and three 60-day appeal deadlines, and they do not run together. Put each one through the check on its own.

Crossing between zones

Driving from one part of the centre to another often means leaving one zone and entering another, and every entry is read by its own camera. Circling to find a hotel entrance can trigger several in under an hour. That is not a malfunction and it is not being charged twice for one thing. Each entry is its own violation with its own verbale.

The exit-gate trap

Rome has been adding cameras that read vehicles on the way out as well as in. That catches people who entered perfectly legally before the restriction began: if you are still inside when the zone closes and you then drive out through a monitored gate, the exit itself is recorded. Parking inside a zone for the afternoon and leaving in the evening is exactly the pattern.

The hotel question

Hotels inside a zone can register a guest's plate so the entry counts as authorised. Whether it happens depends entirely on the hotel doing it, and doing it in time. If you sent reception your plate and have their reply in writing, that is a ground worth appealing on. If the exchange happened at the desk with nothing written down, it is a story, not a ground.

Where a Rome appeal goes

To the Prefecture of Rome, in Via IV Novembre, within 60 days of the date you were notified. The generator fills that address in for you, along with the certified email address, so you are not left hunting for it.
