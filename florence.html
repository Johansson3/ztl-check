A ZTL fine from Florence

Florence produces more confused foreign drivers than any other Italian city, and usually for one of two reasons: the hotel was supposed to register the plate and something went wrong, or the driver came back at eleven at night in July, when the zone they thought had closed at eight was open again.

What "the hotel will sort it" actually means

A hotel inside the zone can register a guest's plate so the entry is treated as authorised. It is not automatic and it is not instant. Someone at reception has to enter your plate, within the window the city allows, and they have to get it right.

Three things go wrong routinely: nobody enters it at all; it is entered after the window has closed; or a character is wrong, which means both you and the owner of the real plate now have a problem.

The appeal lives or dies on one email If you sent your plate to the hotel in writing and have their reply, this is a real ground. If the exchange happened at the desk with nothing in writing, there is nothing to prove, and appealing will cost you more than paying. Why a failed appeal costs more.

The summer night zone

Through the year the sectors of the historic centre are restricted on weekdays and Saturday daytime. But from early April to early October, Florence adds a night restriction on Thursday, Friday and Saturday nights, running from late evening until the small hours.

This is the one that catches visitors. You check the hours, see that the ZTL ends in the evening, drive in at midnight on a Friday in July, and are fined. Nothing about the daytime sign tells you the night rule exists. If that is your situation, the timing is not a ground, but it is worth knowing why it happened.

Some gates are closed around the clock

A handful of entrances are reserved for buses and emergency vehicles and are restricted 24 hours a day, every day. There is no hour at which driving through one of them is permitted, so "the ZTL was not active" cannot be argued for those gates.

What the fine costs

Amounts vary by the specific violation and are set by the comune, with the statutory costs of assessment and service added on top. Paying within five days of notification takes 30% off, but paying also ends your right to appeal, so decide before you pay rather than after.

If you have the email

Keep the whole thread, timestamps included, and the booking confirmation showing your dates. Those two together are the appeal. Add the rental agreement if the car was hired, because it proves which plate was actually yours.

If you do not

Check the letter for a factual error instead: plate, date, time, make of car. Any of those is a far stronger ground than the hotel story. Signage is sometimes argued too, where a sign is obscured by a tree or placed so late that a driver cannot act on it, but that is a harder case and it needs photographs.

Where a Florence appeal goes

To the Prefecture of Florence, in Via Cavour, within 60 days of the date you were notified. The generator fills in that address for you.
